# TRANSITX — Problem Statement

## Academic Context
- **Course**: Data Science Ecosystem (23CS2024)
- **Academic Level**: 3rd Year B.Tech Computer Science and Engineering
- **Focus**: Urban Public Transportation Data Analytics & Machine Learning

---

## 1. The Core Challenge
Public transportation systems generate immense volumes of operational data every day—including passenger taps, schedule timetables, headway variations, corridor transfers, and vehicle locations.

However, for transit planners, city authorities, and the public:
> *"It can be difficult to quickly understand when passenger demand becomes unusually high, which stations become overcrowded, where delays are more common, and how travel patterns shift across days and hours."*

## 2. Key Analytical Questions
1. **Demand Spikes**: Which transit corridors experience peak passenger crowding, and when does demand surge?
2. **Station Congestion**: At what hours do interchange stations reach critical platform occupancy?
3. **Diurnal Fluctuations**: How does passenger flow fluctuate between morning commuter rushes, midday lulls, evening return rushes, and weekends?
4. **Operational Bottlenecks**: Which routes suffer the highest headway variance against published schedules?
5. **Statistical Anomalies**: How can transit operators differentiate routine rush-hour peaks from true statistical demand surges (such as event crowds or weather impacts)?
6. **Corridor Grouping**: Which routes share similar operational behavior, ridership scale, and delay profiles?

## 3. Communication Standard
This project avoids overly opaque terminology and communicates insights simply:
- **Instead of**: *"Multivariate spatiotemporal demand characterization"*
- **We state**: *"See how passenger demand changes by place and time."*
